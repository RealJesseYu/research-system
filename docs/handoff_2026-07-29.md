## 2. What we learned — the load-bearing facts

**A study costs ~$53, not ~$270.** Reported "tokens" sum input, output, cache
writes and cache reads at face value; **96.3%** of the glider's 54M were cache
reads at $0.50/M. **Conversion: ~$1 per million reported tokens.** Verified twice.

**Orchestration is 1.3% of spend.** 98.7% is inside experimenter runs. This
deleted "streamline compute" as an item — there is nothing there to win.

**ADDITIVE survives, SUBSTITUTIVE does not.** `study-20260728T225951Z` ($27.50)
tested 7 memory policies in simulation and 5 against a real LLM director. **Every
bounded or summarised memory policy lost to re-reading the whole arc.** The
mechanism: an injected FALSE "already answered" record was re-probed **0.0 times**
under a summary vs **1.67** under full re-read. **The un-summarised arc is the
self-correction mechanism.** Arc compaction is dead; do not revive it without
evidence beating a matched floor.

**Wall-clock serialism, not memory, is the long-run ceiling.** Every move is
69–84 agentic turns and ~1 hour. That is what A1 attacks.

**Goal shape is the biggest single lever on output quality.** The glider study's
90% verification was *commanded* by its own goal text (*"Each part must be
verified before it is built on"*). Never write a standing verification clause.

**The system's own ideation apparatus works and fires when warranted** — three
times across all studies, always when the goal made the ideas the deliverable.
